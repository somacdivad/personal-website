Sometimes your morning read is exactly what you need.
For the fourth day in a row I woke up later than planned, never even hearing my alarm, and my daughter was late to a school program as a result.
When I arrived home from dropping her off, I poured myself a cup of coffee and then let the mug drop onto my kitchen table a bit too hard, spilling a little in the process.

Montaigne's fourth essay is called _How the soul discharges its emotions against false objects when lacking real ones_.
It's strange to be called out by a 16th century French nobleman.

> What causes do we not discover for the ills which befall us!
> What will we not attack, rightly or wrongly, rather than go without something to skirmish against?

But perhaps there is some solace to be found in the fact these violent reactions against imagined foes have been getting the better of us for millenia.
Who is immune from the occasional outburst?
If we can at least recognize our error quickly and acknowledge its futility before we do any real damage, we can redirect that energy towards something productive.

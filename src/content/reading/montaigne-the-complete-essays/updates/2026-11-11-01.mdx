This morning I read Montaigne's third essay *Our emotions get carried away beyond us*. From the title, I expected it to be somewhat similar to his second essay *On sadness*, which discusses the power emotions like sadness and joy have to incapacitate us. But "carried away beyond us" here is referring not to being overwhelmed, but to something entirely external.

Montaigne spends most of the essay examining various historical figures' concerns for their own funeral. It is clear that Montaigne sees these worries as meritless:

> And whoever does know himself never considers external things to be his; and above all other things he loves and cultivates himself: he rejects excessive concerns as well as useless thoughts and resolutions.

I admit that I sometimes struggle with this kind of advice. It is, on the one hand, wise. Far too often I let myself boil over with anger at something that doesn't concern me, or lose sleep over things I can't control. What's worse, these emotions sometimes prevent me from properly attending to my family or to my own mental health.

But on the other hand, it can sometimes feel both selfish and complacent. There are real problems in the world, and as a citizen of a democracy it is my right and my duty to opine on these problems by casting my vote.

Is it possible to cast informed votes while also rejecting excessive concerns and useless thoughts, especially when so many of the issues on which politicians campaign are excessive and useless?

I agree with Montaigne. But it's hard to discern what is excessive and useless.

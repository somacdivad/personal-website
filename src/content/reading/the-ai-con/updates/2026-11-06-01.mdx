Chapter 3 is titled "Leisure for Me, Gig Work for Thee: The AI Hype At Work."
Reading that title, I thought of Aristotle's notion that leisure is a requirement for the development of virtue and a necessary condition for flourishing.
Only the ruling class could enjoy this leisure, however, since it was predicated on the labor of tradesmen, artisans, and slaves.

Yet we see in the many examples given in this chapter that the "virtues" developed through the leisure enjoyed by leaders of the tech industry look less like wisdom and more like debauchery and debasement.

There are too many examples to list here, and some are too grotesque and egregious to recount in detail.
But one stood out to me as a particularly striking example of the tech industry's moral bankruptcy: Amazon's [Mechanical Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Mechanical_Turk) program.
This platform paid dismal wages to workers who performed "microtasks" so that tech companies could harvest data needed to train AI systems.

The name "Mechanical Turk" is a reference to an [18th-century machine that could play chess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Turk).
It was later revealed to be a hoax.
The "automation" was merely a human hidden inside the machine using magnets to manipulate the chess pieces.

That Amazon chose this name for its platform is revealing.
They know that what they are presenting as "automation" is an illusion built on the labor of hidden human workers.
Why would they so openly admit this?
Do they believe we are so naive? Are they signalling to other tech companies? Is it tech-bro dick-swinging?

Whatever the reason, we can only conclude that exploitation is not an accident or an oversight.
It's a deliberate strategy.
